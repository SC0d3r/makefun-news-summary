World leaders gathered in Antalya, Turkey, for the COP31 climate summit, where host Turkey pressed for a new climate agreement even as several major figures stayed away (Reuters, AFP).

In Abu Dhabi, US-brokered talks between Russia and Ukraine ran into a second day, with Volodymyr Zelensky saying he was pleased by a shift in Washington's approach and envoy Jared Kushner claiming the gaps had been narrowed, while Kyiv kept demanding security guarantees (AFP). Millions of Spanish workers walked off the job in the country's first general strike in 14 years, protesting a housing crisis that has nearly doubled average rents in a decade, less than three weeks before an election that could hand power to the far right (AP).

Financial markets were rattled by a strong US inflation reading that stoked fears of an outsized Federal Reserve rate increase, sending global bonds lower and keeping the dollar firm; gold recovered slightly after a slide of almost 3%, and crude rose as attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz undercut optimism about an Iran truce (Reuters). In US politics, the midterm results left the pro-Israel lobby AIPAC on the back foot, with Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed offering mocking thanks and James Talarico saying he had forsworn its money (AFP). The controversy surrounding Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang revived calls in Washington for a harder line on China (AFP).

Elsewhere, a public bus struck several vehicles in Bundang, South Korea, seriously injuring nine people among 48 hurt (Reuters), and the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo passed 11,000 cases and 5,387 deaths (Al Jazeera).
